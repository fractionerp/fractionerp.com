**Who this article is for:** Business owners, managers and team members who already use an ERP system but suspect they are not making full use of it. You are past the initial purchase and implementation stage and now the system is part of everyday work. The question now is whether you are getting enough from what you have already paid for.

## The Ferrari in Second Gear

When you introduce a new computer system, you are unlikely to master it on day one. An ERP system is no exception. People learn the functions they need to get through their work, build habits around them and move on.

Meanwhile, the system may have much more to offer. That is especially true of cloud software, which continues to develop after you start using it. Features can appear or improve without changing the way your team works—simply because nobody has noticed them.

It is a bit like driving a Ferrari in second gear. The vehicle still moves, but you are not discovering what it can really do. Your ERP system may be doing its job while leaving useful functionality unexplored.

## Make Time to Poke Around

The remedy does not have to begin with a major project. Put a small block of time in your diary to explore the system you already have. Once a month or once a quarter could be enough to build the habit; even a few minutes is a start.

Browse the menus. Look for functions you do not recognise. Check whether anything has appeared since you last took a proper look. This is not about changing settings at random or adopting every feature you find. It is about becoming aware of your options.

You do not have to explore the whole system alone, either. Different colleagues could look at different areas on different dates. Between them, they may uncover functions that are particularly relevant to their day-to-day work.

## Ask Whether It Actually Helps

Finding a feature is only the first step. Use your exploration time to understand what it does, then ask a practical question: would using it make work better or worse?

A feature may sound impressive but add little value to your business. Another may remove a small frustration that your team has quietly accepted for years. The aim is not to use more of the system for its own sake. It is to find the functions that help people work more productively and effectively.

If something looks promising, talk to the colleagues who would use it. Spend a little time working out how it would fit the way your business wants to operate. Then, if it genuinely helps, adopt it properly rather than leaving it as an interesting discovery.

## Get More from What You Already Own

An ERP system is an investment, not a fixed set of habits. If you never revisit how you use it, you may miss opportunities sitting inside the software you already bought.

You do not need to master every function at once. Start by making space to look, ask questions and test whether a feature is useful. Repeat that a few times a year and encourage others to do the same.

The goal of this article is straightforward: get the best return from your system and, hopefully, make the working day a little easier.
