Why Bar Stock Is Different from Ordinary Inventory

For most components, total quantity is enough. For bar, tube, extrusion and other linear material, it can give a dangerously incomplete answer.

The total length can look right while the job is still short

Imagine that your ERP shows 5.20 metres of a material in stock. That sounds enough for a 1.50 metre requirement. But the total is made from three physical pieces: 3.00 m, 1.35 m and 0.85 m. Only one of them can supply the complete cut.

A conventional stock balance adds the lengths together. It does not understand that two short offcuts cannot be joined to make one longer component. This is the central bar stock problem: available length and usable length are not the same thing.

The same issue affects round bar, flat bar, square bar, tube, pipe, structural sections and extrusions whenever production consumes a defined cut length from a physical piece.

Where Bar Stock Control Breaks Down

Many engineering businesses compensate with labels, notebooks or a separate offcut spreadsheet. The information soon drifts away from live production.

False availability

The system has enough total length, but no single piece can provide the required cut.

Invisible cutting loss

The BOM consumes the product length but ignores material removed by the saw blade or cutting process.

Forgotten offcuts

Usable remnants build up because nobody can quickly see their lengths, locations or material identity.

Broken traceability

A remnant loses its connection to the original receipt, supplier information or heat and cast details.

Unnecessary purchasing

New bars are ordered because production cannot trust or easily search what is already on the rack.

Difficult stock counts

A total metre figure cannot be checked properly without measuring every physical piece.

One identity per physical piece

Receive and trace every bar separately

At goods receipt, Fraction creates an individual identity for each bar. The received length, remaining usable length, stock location and receipt reference stay with that piece. Several bars can share the same heat or cast number without being collapsed into one anonymous balance.

Reserve material the shop floor can use

Allocate complete cuts, not an abstract length

The BOM and works order use a cut length for a linear-stock component. Fraction checks the available length on each physical piece, includes the kerf for every cut and reserves only complete cuts that the selected bar can supply.

Automatic allocation considers remnants first and then chooses the smallest suitable piece. This helps consume usable offcuts before opening a full bar, without allocating a cut across two pieces. If circumstances change, an untouched allocation can be released and rebuilt from current stock.

Keep the rack and the ERP aligned

Count by measuring, not guessing

Linear-stock counts present each physical piece for measurement. Enter the actual remaining length for every bar or offcut, and Fraction records the adjustment against that piece and its location.

Open work-order reservations are protected: a piece cannot be reduced below the length already committed to active cuts. A zero measurement records a piece as physically absent or fully scrapped rather than silently leaving phantom stock available.

What “Best Fit” Means in Fraction ERP

The aim is practical, dependable ERP allocation: use known remnants where possible and ensure every planned cut fits one physical piece.

What Fraction does

  • Checks the measured length of individual bars and offcuts

  • Prioritises remnants before full bars
  • Selects the smallest suitable piece within that priority

Bar Stock Management FAQs

What is bar stock management software?

Bar stock management software records linear raw material by physical piece and usable length. It helps an engineering business purchase bars, receive and identify them, allocate complete cuts to work orders, account for cutting loss, retain offcuts and reconcile measured stock.

Why is a total metre balance not enough?

A total does not show whether one piece is long enough for the required component. Three offcuts can add up to more than the demand while every individual offcut is too short. Piece-level records make that constraint visible.

How does Fraction ERP manage offcuts?

After a cut, the remaining usable length stays against the same individually tracked piece. Fraction classifies it as a remnant, keeps its receipt and traceability context, and considers it before full bars during automatic best-fit allocation.

Does Fraction ERP include kerf in material usage?

Yes. A default kerf can be set on the linear-stock part and overridden on a BOM or works-order line. Fraction reserves and consumes the product length plus the kerf for each complete cut.

Can Fraction retain heat or cast traceability?

Yes. Each received bar can carry its own heat or cast reference while several bars may share the same reference. The piece remains connected to its goods-receipt and supplier context as its usable length changes.

Can I stock-count bars and offcuts individually?

Yes. Fraction presents every active piece for measurement and records the variance against that identity and stock location. It also protects quantities reserved to open work orders.
